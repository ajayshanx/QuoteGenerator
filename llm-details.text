Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI systems trained on massive amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like language.

Key details:
- They are typically built using transformer architectures.
- They predict the next token in a sequence during training and generation.
- They can perform tasks such as summarization, translation, coding assistance, question answering, and content generation.
- Their performance depends on model size, training data quality, alignment methods, and inference setup.
- Common limitations include hallucinations, bias, and context-window constraints.

How they are used:
- Prompting: users provide instructions or examples.
- Fine-tuning: models are adapted for domain-specific tasks.
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): external knowledge is fetched and combined with generation for more grounded responses.

Why they matter:
LLMs improve productivity, automate repetitive language tasks, and enable natural-language interfaces for software and data systems. :)
